Net jobs added outside farming, plus the unemployment rate and average hourly earnings, all in one 13:30 UK release on the first Friday of the month.

Why gold cares
Labour market strength is the other half of the Fed's mandate. A strong report pushes rate-cut expectations out and pressures gold; a weak one brings cuts forward and supports it.
What to watch
Three numbers land simultaneously and can disagree: the payroll count, the unemployment rate, and average hourly earnings. Revisions to the prior two months often matter more than the headline.
The catch
The most whipsaw-prone release on the calendar. The first move is frequently wrong, and the first fifteen minutes routinely reverse.

Escalation→Oil supply risk→Real yields→Gold
Why it reaches markets
The one flashpoint that hits gold through every channel at once. Safe-haven bid on the headline, Treasuries bid so nominal and real yields fall, and - uniquely - a credible threat to the Strait of Hormuz spikes crude, which lifts inflation expectations. Roughly a fifth of seaborne oil passes through that strait.
Working against it
The dollar is also a haven, so risk-off bids the dollar too and cancels part of gold's gain. This is why some escalation days produce a far smaller gold move than the headlines imply.
How long it lasts
Days. Gold spikes on the exchange of fire and gives most of it back within a week unless the oil disruption is real and sustained, because only then does it reach the Fed's reaction function.
