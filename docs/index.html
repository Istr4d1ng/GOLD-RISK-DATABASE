Event briefings

What each release is, why gold cares, and what it has actually done here before.

13:30 Non-Farm Payrolls

Published together with Average Hourly Earnings m/m, Unemployment Rate — one release, one reaction.

Net jobs added outside farming, plus the unemployment rate and average hourly earnings, all in one 13:30 UK release on the first Friday of the month.

Why gold cares
Labour market strength is the other half of the Fed's mandate. A strong report pushes rate-cut expectations out and pressures gold; a weak one brings cuts forward and supports it.
What to watch
Three numbers land simultaneously and can disagree: the payroll count, the unemployment rate, and average hourly earnings. Revisions to the prior two months often matter more than the headline.
The catch
The most whipsaw-prone release on the calendar. The first move is frequently wrong, and the first fifteen minutes routinely reverse.
Rough prior
$20-50 within the hour

No measured history yet — the figures above are a prior, not a statistic.

15:00 UoM Consumer Sentiment

Michigan's household survey, released in preliminary and revised form at 15:00 UK.

Why gold cares
The sentiment number is minor. The inflation-expectations component attached to it is not.
What to watch
The 1-year and 5-10 year inflation expectations. The Fed watches the long-run figure closely, so a jump there is a genuine rate signal.
The catch
Small sample, frequently revised, and prone to reflecting petrol prices more than anything else.

$3-12
