Event briefings

What each release is, why gold cares, and what it has actually done here before.

12:30 GDP

Quarterly economic growth, annualised. Released 13:30 UK in three passes - advance, second, third.

Why gold cares
Matters for the growth half of the picture, but it is backward-looking by up to four months.
What to watch
The advance reading is the only one that reliably moves anything. Later revisions rarely register.
The catch
Gold typically shrugs off GDP unless the miss is large or it confirms an existing recession narrative.
Rough prior
$5-15

12:30 Core PCE Price Index

The Fed's preferred inflation gauge, stripped of food and energy. Released 13:30 UK.

Why gold cares
It is the measure the Fed's 2% target is actually written against, so it carries more policy weight than CPI even though it moves markets less.
What to watch
The m/m figure to two decimal places, and the three- and six-month annualised runs that commentators compute from it.
The catch
Largely predictable from the CPI and PPI released earlier in the month, so the surprise is usually small - which is exactly why a surprise here hits hard.
Rough prior
$10-30

No measured history yet — the figures above are a prior, not a statistic.

12:30 Initial Jobless Claims

New unemployment benefit filings in the prior week. Released every Thursday at 13:30 UK.

Why gold cares
The highest-frequency labour signal available. Individually minor, but a trend of rising claims shifts the whole rate narrative.
What to watch
The four-week moving average rather than the weekly print, and continuing claims for whether people are finding new work.
The catch
Distorted by holidays, weather and state-level administrative quirks. One bad week means nothing.
Rough prior
$3-10

Escalation→Oil supply risk→Real yields→Gold
Why it reaches markets
The one flashpoint that hits gold through every channel at once. Safe-haven bid on the headline, Treasuries bid so nominal and real yields fall, and - uniquely - a credible threat to the Strait of Hormuz spikes crude, which lifts inflation expectations. Roughly a fifth of seaborne oil passes through that strait.
Working against it
The dollar is also a haven, so risk-off bids the dollar too and cancels part of gold's gain. This is why some escalation days produce a far smaller gold move than the headlines imply.
How long it lasts
Days. Gold spikes on the exchange of fire and gives most of it back within a week unless the oil disruption is real and sustained, because only then does it reach the Fed's reaction function.
